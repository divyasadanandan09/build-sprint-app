1. The job

Instructor job (the one we serve first):
When a client tells the instructor she's loving the classes, the instructor wants to turn that into a recommendation the client posts in her own society WhatsApp group, so she can get enquiries from neighbours who ignore her flyers and her own posts.

Client job:
When the client is seeing results from classes she enjoys and the instructor asks how it's going, she wants to recommend the instructor to her neighbours without having to sit down and write something, so she can help someone she likes and be the neighbour who found a good thing.

Done, in the instructor's terms: the client has posted a recommendation for the instructor's classes in her society group. For this sprint, nothing else counts as done.

Why week 4: the instructor can't keep track of which clients are happy at any given time. Week 4 is when the product checks, because that's usually when results start to show. It's the trigger for the job, not the job itself.

Other moments it happens: a client finishes 1 or 2 dance workshops, a client hits a visible milestone (her first full class without a break, a few kilos down), or a client has already left the instructor a Google review.

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): an instructor whose happy clients live within walking distance of hundreds of homes, but whose own posts in the society group get no replies.

Today they hire: blanket requests in the clients' WhatsApp group, sent to everyone with occasional reminders, plus the odd ask after class. Happy clients agree, then never write anything. The few who do leave a review on her Google Business Page, where neighbours scrolling their society group never see it.

(Advanced, optional) What needs doing:

Keep the two commitments separate. The check-in is private feedback. Only a happy client gets a second, explicit ask to recommend publicly, and she can say no.
When a reply is too thin to write from, ask one easy question about what's changed for her.
When a client is unhappy, pass it to the instructor privately and stop. No recommendation ask.

(Advanced, optional) How they want to feel:

Instructor: relieved. She never has to remember who to ask, chase anyone, or think up what to write.
Client: proud of her progress, not put on the spot.

(Advanced, optional) How they want to look to others:

Client: genuine, a neighbour sharing something that worked, not someone advertising.
Instructor: professional and in demand, not pushy.

(B2B only) The bench: the end user is the client; the decision maker and payer is the instructor.
The one we serve first: the instructor.

2. The switch

What they'd fire: blanket testimonial requests in the clients' WhatsApp group, ad hoc asks after class, and the instructor's own promo posts in society groups that get ignored.

The forces that matter
Push, outside them (what's going wrong in their life right now): the instructor's posts and flyers bring almost no enquiries. Chasing clients for testimonials takes time, and the reviews she does get stay on Google, where her neighbours don't see them.
Pull, inside them (what they've always wanted): neighbours who trust her craft enough to enquire, and a studio filled from the homes within 1 km.
Habit (the way they already do it): asking everyone at once in the group and hoping someone responds, and promoting herself.

What the product does about each:

Push: it tells the instructor who to ask today and writes the ask, so there's nothing to chase. Her existing Google reviews become recommendations in the groups where her neighbours actually are.
Pull: the recommendation lands in a neighbour's group from someone they already know, not from the instructor.
Habit: it stays inside WhatsApp, on the instructor's own number. She taps send instead of writing.

The one worry onboarding must remove: the instructor worries that asking clients to promote her will feel pushy, and that using an app will make it feel fake. Every message goes from her own WhatsApp in her voice, the client only posts if she agrees, and the words are the client's own.

3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before your product):
The instructor posts a blanket ask in the clients' group > a few clients say they'll write something > she sends reminders > most never write > the few who do leave a Google review that never reaches a society group.

[where it ends today] A few reviews on her Google Business Page, none of them in any society group.

With my product:

A client hits week 4 (or finishes a workshop). The product nudges the instructor: "Priya hits week 4 today. Tap to send."
The instructor taps. Her WhatsApp opens with the check-in already written in her voice, and she sends it.
The client replies by voice note or text, in any language.
The instructor forwards the reply to the agent.
If the client is happy, the agent drafts a neighbour-style message from the client's own words, plus a short ask to share it. If the reply is too thin, it drafts one follow-up question first. If the client is unhappy, it flags this to the instructor privately and stops.
The instructor sends the draft to the client: "Would you be okay sharing this in your society group?"
[last] [the job done, in their terms]: the client forwards it to her society group, and the instructor's classes have a recommendation from a neighbour.

Things it takes to get the job done today: 4+, and it rarely reaches done · With my product: 3 taps for the instructor, 2 actions for the client

What must not happen:

Step 5: never send the recommendation ask to an unhappy client. Route her to the instructor privately.
Step 1: never check in before the minimum period for that program (4 weeks by default).
Step 5: never add results or claims the client didn't make. The message uses only what she said.
Step 7: never post anything on the client's behalf. She decides whether to share and where.
Steps 3 and 6: never send more than one gentle reminder if the client doesn't reply.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered):

Unhappy client: the feedback goes to the instructor only, with no ask.
Client declines to post: the instructor thanks her and stops. Her feedback still reaches the instructor.
Thin reply: one follow-up question, then draft from what's there.
No reply: one gentle reminder after a few days, then stop.
Existing Google review: the instructor pastes it in, and the flow starts at step 5.
4. Onboarding

First value (the moment it first does the job for them): within her first minute, the instructor pastes one Google review a client has already left her. She gets back a neighbour-style WhatsApp message in that client's words, plus a message in her own voice asking the client to share it in her society group. The first value is the moment she reads the draft and thinks "I'd send this." The client posting it comes later and is the job done.

The smallest commitment we ask for: one review she already has, copied and pasted. No client list, no settings.

The worry it removes (from section 2): that asking will feel pushy or fake. She sees that the draft uses only the client's words, and that the ask is polite and in her voice, before anything goes out.

From opening the link to the first value:

The instructor opens a WhatsApp link to the agent. It says: "Paste one Google review a client left you, or forward something a happy client said."
She pastes a review.
Within seconds she gets two messages: the neighbour-style recommendation in the client's words, and the ask to send that client.
She taps to send it to the client. That's the first small proof. The post follows when the client is ready.

Login: not in v1. Her WhatsApp number is her account, and everything she shares is saved against it from the first message.

Where the agent lives: its own WhatsApp number on the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API). The daily nudge is a Meta-approved utility template, since the agent messages her first.

What we don't ask on day one: her full client list, a wait period for each program (it defaults to 4 weeks), access to her contact list, her pricing, client notes, Instagram.

What we ask later, and when:

Owner update, 6 Oct: do not automatically ask for the next client after drafts. The instructor initiates client additions when she chooses by sending "New client Ananya joined on 12 Sept"; the earlier name/date format also remains accepted. She can introduce a review conversationally with "This is a review", on its own or before the review.
After that: the rest of her clients, one at a time as they approach week 4, and her other Google reviews, each of which becomes another ready ask.
When she adds a workshop or short-program client: the wait period, but only if 4 weeks doesn't fit.
After the first week: per-client notes that shift the timing.
After the first recommendations go out: how each new enquiry heard about her.
5. v1

Does (the must haves: one person finishes the one job):

Turns a pasted Google review or a forwarded client message into a neighbour-style draft, plus the ask to share it, within a minute.
Tracks each client's start date and flags who's due today.
Writes the check-in in the instructor's voice, ready to send from her WhatsApp.
Reads client replies as voice notes or text, in any language.
Sorts happy clients from unhappy ones, and asks one follow-up when a reply is thin.
Drafts every message using only the client's own words.

Doesn't (not this sprint: parked, not forgotten): collecting new Google reviews, Instagram content, a testimonial library with tags, the agent messaging clients directly (it only ever talks to the instructor; clients hear only from her own WhatsApp), tracking which enquiry came from which post, brand-language templates.

Nice to have (only after the must haves work): a "How did you hear about us?" question at demo booking to link enquiries to recommendations, and milestone triggers beyond week 4.

How I'll know it worked (what they do again, not what they say): in week 2, the instructor acts on the nudges for 3 or more clients without being reminded. She keeps using it after the first month, and at least one new enquiry mentions a neighbour's post.
